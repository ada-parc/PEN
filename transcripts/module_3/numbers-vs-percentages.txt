Welcome back to Module 3. In this unit, we'll look at how to read numbers and percentages. Both are useful, but they tell us different stories. Sometimes we see disability information as numbers and sometimes as percentages. Understanding the difference helps us explain the story in a clear way. Numbers tell us how many. For example, 500 people with disabilities have jobs. But this doesn't tell us much by itself. Percentages tell us how big a part.

For example, 20% of people with disabilities have jobs. This helps us compare different cities or groups. When we compare different cities or states, the size of the population is not the same. That's why we always start with the percentages. Percentages let us compare fairly, no matter how big or small the city is. After that, we can use numbers to give us the exact details for our own city or state. So remember, percentages first for fairness, numbers second for detail.

This shows us the exact count. How many people are working? Next, look at the percentage of people with disabilities who are employed in your state. This shows us how big a part of the community is working, compared to the whole population. Finally, compare your state to another state. Let's compare two states. In Illinois, about 1.48 million people have disabilities. That's 11.8% of the population. In Mississippi, about 500,000 people have disabilities. That's 17.4% of the population. Even though Illinois has more people with disabilities in total, the percentage is higher in Mississippi. This shows us why percentages matter when we compare places. When we compare different places, always start with percentages.

Percentages give us the bigger picture and make the comparison fair. Then, use numbers to show the exact details for your own community. Numbers tell us how many. Percentages tell us how big a part. When we put them together, we tell a clear and stronger story about disability in our communities. Thank you.
