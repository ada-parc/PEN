Welcome to the final unit of Module 3. In this unit, we will learn how to understand a scorecard. Hopefully, this term sounds familiar as we covered scorecards in Module 2. So, let's review. What does a scorecard mean? The scorecard on the ADA-PARC website shows how people with disabilities are doing in different parts of life. It uses data from the 2023 census collected through the American Community Survey, ACS, which is conducted by the U.S. Census Bureau.

The scorecard makes numbers easier to see and compare, like a report card for a community. It helps us spot where things are going well and where changes are needed. Scores are shown in two ways, from 0 to 100 in Q1 to Q4. The scorecard uses a grade from 0 to 100. A score of 0 is much worse than average. A state is doing poorly compared to others. A score of 50 is average, the middle, right in line where most states are scoring.

A score of 100 is much better than average. A state is doing great compared to others. The scorecard also uses quartiles from Q1 to Q4. Q1 is the worst scores, poor opportunities for people with disabilities. Q2 is below average. Q3 is above average. And Q4 is the best scores, the greatest opportunity for people with disabilities. Let's look at an example. The community participation score shows how well each state supports people with disabilities in everyday community life.

States are grouped into four quartiles, shown by color. Q4 is that dark red. These are the highest scores, meaning the best opportunities for people with disabilities. Example states include Minnesota, Oregon, and Washington. Q3 is the red color. This is above average opportunities. Example states include New York, Pennsylvania, and Colorado. Q2 is orange, and this is below average opportunities.

Example states include Florida, Texas, and Kentucky. Q1 is yellow. These are the lowest scores, meaning the least opportunities for people with disabilities. Example states include Alabama, Mississippi, and Louisiana. This scorecard helps compare states, see where participation is strong, and notice where more support is needed. Now it's your turn.
