And how do we read a scorecard? Data used to develop ADA-PARC scorecards derives from federally recognized sources, the American Community Survey, ACS. Each year, the ACS collects local individual data. The ACS is a survey done every year in the United States. It asks questions about things like housing, jobs, health, and transportation. With this survey, we can compare people with and without disabilities.

This helps us see the differences between the two groups. Here is a link to the ACS website. The ACA is important because it gives us a snapshot of who lives in our communities and what challenges they face. For example, in my state, fewer people with disabilities have jobs compared to people without disabilities. How does the ACS define disability? It defines it in six categories. These definitions help us focus on how disability affects everyday activities, not just diagnoses.

So, seeing. A person may have serious trouble seeing even with glasses or being blind. Hearing. So, serious trouble hearing or being deaf. Mobility. Trouble walking or climbing stairs. May use a wheelchair or a cane. Thinking and remembering. So, cognitive. Trouble concentrating, remembering, or making decisions. Self-care. So, trouble with daily tasks like dressing or bathing.

And living independently. Trouble running errands alone like shopping or visiting a doctor. The six categories are only a starting point. Disability is much more diverse than this. If you need more details, you can look at other sources like the Census Bureau website. But for our purposes, we'll use these six categories to keep things simple and useful.
